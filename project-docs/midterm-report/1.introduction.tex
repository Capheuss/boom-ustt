\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

 RISC-V security is still an open area, with many questions about how to defend against 
 Spectre attacks. Even though gem5 simulation has validated the security of many defenses, 
 their area and performance costs in real hardware remain unclear.
 Recent work has shown that commercial RISC-V cores are still vulnerable to Spectre, 
 which has prompted efforts to systematically classify software and hardware defenses for 
 RISC-V~\cite{Gerlach:SpectreClass:2026}. Speculative Taint Tracking
 (STT)~\cite{Yu:STT:2019} is one such defense, evaluated on x86 and ARM, and
 $\mu$STT~\cite{Chen:uSTT:2025} reduces its hardware cost.

 This raises three questions: can STT and its $\mu$STT refinements be built into 
 a complete RISC-V core, what do they cost in performance, area and timing, and 
 do the results published for their gem5 and standalone RTL evaluations still 
 hold in a full design?
