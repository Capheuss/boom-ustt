\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

% Owner: Samir Joshi (@SamirJoshiGit). Tracking: GitHub issue #26.
% Carries over to the final report.

This project implements Speculative Taint Tracking and the two $\mu$STT mechanisms,
the Age Matrix and the impede micro-op, in the RTL of SonicBOOM. Until now, $\mu$STT
has only been evaluated in gem5 and as a standalone rename circuit, and neither
evaluation ran any attacks. By the end of the project, we want to see three results.
First, the BOOM Spectre proofs of concept should recover the secret on the baseline
core and fail on every protected configuration. Second, the performance cost, measured
as cycles and IPC in Verilator and normalized to the baseline, should fall between the
baseline and the conservative stand-in, with $\mu$STT costing slightly more than STT
as it does in gem5. Third, the Vivado reports should show the area, timing and power
cost of each mechanism inside a complete core, and synthesizing the released $\mu$STT
circuit at BOOM's parameters should tell us whether its area and delay claims hold.
Together, these results will show whether $\mu$STT works as a practical Spectre
defense in real hardware.
